Focusing on the Real World

How should I navigate conversational rabbit holes?

Having conversations about the real world in your math classroom, means opening up to all of the related and connecting thoughts your students may wish to share on the topic. Have your students ever pulled your lesson into a conversational tangent that ate up all of your class time and best intentions?

  • Which student comments show that they're activating prior experiences or knowledge relating to this lesson? What are some comments that derail or detract from the conversation?
  • How do some of the teacher's responses support or hinder the following goals they have for this lesson launch?

  • Conversations about the real-world can elicit unexpected comments that have the potential to veer the  discussion in unexpected directions. What are some different types of rabbit holes that you may encounter while talking about the world? How do you decide whether to reign in a conversation or to go with the flow?
  • When a teacher does want to redirect the conversation, what are some ways they can move on while still honoring the student who shared the comment or question?
  • Consider a recent time the conversation in your own classroom slipped down a rabbit hole (for better or worse).  How did this impact the lesson?
  • What new moves would you like to explore for navigating around or through rabbit holes in your next real world conversation? What will you need to do while preparing a lesson to prepare to try this?
  • What is one small thing you can do tomorrow in support of those shifts?
